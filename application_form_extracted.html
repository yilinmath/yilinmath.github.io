Abstract


This conference is the seventh of a series of international conferences devoted to Hochschild cohomology with applications in Algebra, Geometry, Topology and Mathematical Physics, previous editions held in BIRS Canada,  CIRM France, Buenos Aires Argentina,  Oberwalfach  Germany etc.  The conference will bring together researchers working on Hochschild cohomology, deformation theory, noncommutative geometry, representation theory, homological mirror symmetry and algebraic topology, fostering interdisciplinary dialogue and collaboration. Emphasis will be placed on recent advances, open problems, and emerging connections to other research fields.

Description of the aim


Hochschild cohomology, initially developed as a tool for associative algebras, has evolved into a versatile and powerful invariant that threads through diverse branches of modern mathematics. Its core lies in measuring the "noncommutative geometry" of an algebra, probing its deformation theory, smoothness, and hidden structure through a homological lens. Originally applied to algebraic deformation problems—where its second group classifies infinitesimal deformations—it became the formal foundation for Kontsevich's deformation quantization, bridging Poisson geometry and quantum algebras.

In algebraic and noncommutative geometry, the Hochschild–Kostant–Rosenberg theorem establishes a profound link to classical geometry, identifying Hochschild homology of a smooth algebra with its module of differential forms. This connection allows Hochschild theory to serve as a substitute for de Rham noncommutative geometry program. In representation theory, it acts as a derived invariant, distinguishing algebras up to derived equivalence and offering insights through support varieties and connections to Lie theory.

The most striking applications arise in topology and mathematical physics, particularly through string theory and mirror symmetry. Here, Hochschild cohomology of an appropriate category —such as the Fukaya category of a symplectic manifold—encodes the algebra of open strings, while its counterpart on the mirror side captures closed-string invariants like quantum cohomology. This correspondence lies at the heart of homological mirror symmetry, positioning Hochschild cohomology as a critical bridge between symplectic and algebraic geometry. In topological field theories, it further structures the space of observables and classifies boundary conditions, demonstrating its unifying role across abstract algebra, geometry, and theoretical physics.

The conference will bring together researchers working on Hochschild cohomology, deformation theory, noncommutative geometry, representation theory, homological mirror symmetry and algebraic topology, fostering interdisciplinary dialogue and collaboration. Emphasis will be placed on recent advances, open problems, and emerging connections to other research fields.
